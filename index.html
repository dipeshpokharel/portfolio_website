01PROFILE / THE HUMAN LAYER

ENGINEERING IS MY FOUNDATION.

Resilience is
the bigger system.

A map can show where danger is. Good risk work helps explain what that danger means for people — and what they can do next.

My path connects civil engineering, disaster risk management and applied data. From reconstruction and settlement planning to last-mile communication and impact-based forecasting, I’m interested in the space between technical knowledge and decisions on the ground.

I believe the next generation of resilience will come from combining local knowledge, credible evidence, usable technology and institutions that can act.

04FIELD SIGNAL / A POINT OF VIEW
RISK≠LOSSTHE HUMAN COST IS THE STORY.

A disaster is more than
a hazard footprint.

It is a household’s savings, a road that no longer connects a market, a school interrupted, a livelihood pushed into uncertainty. Physical damage is visible. Economic disruption can continue long after the floodwater recedes.

That is why I’m drawn to the intersection of disaster risk and economics: to understand not only what was damaged, but what people lose, how they adapt and which investments make recovery more durable.
