Engineering is the start.

My work is about making technical information useful to the people who need it most—local governments, disaster management teams and communities exposed to hazards.

With a background in civil engineering and a master’s-level foundation in disaster risk management, I bring together field observation, spatial analysis, risk communication and coordination. I’m especially interested in how better data and public systems can help people act earlier and recover stronger.

04BEYOND THE MAP

Every disaster has a human and economic story.

Field visits remind me that a hazard is never just a line on a map or a number in a report. It can mean lost livelihoods, interrupted access, damaged public infrastructure and years of household investment wiped away. Good risk work must connect physical impacts with people’s choices, local economies and the systems that help communities recover.

A perspective shaped by engineering, disaster risk management and a growing interest in economics.
